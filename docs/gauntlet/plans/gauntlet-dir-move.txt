## What is wrong

`readable()` at `.claude/hooks/no-impl-reads.py:169` scores one resolved path. It tests the approved specs first at `:206` — "if _under(rel, GAUNTLET_SPECS)" — the gauntlet base second at `:208` — "if _under(rel, GAUNTLET_BASE)" — and the allow list last at `:212-218`. The constants are `GAUNTLET_BASE = "docs/gauntlet"` at `:88` and `GAUNTLET_SPECS = "docs/gauntlet/specs"` at `:90`, and the allow list is `DEFAULT_ALLOW = ("docs/", "tests/", "state/")` at `:86`.

The denied subtree therefore sits inside an allowed one. An ancestor of the denied tree passes all three tests, because it is `docs/` and nothing else. Measured against HEAD (`4b773d1`), each command fed to the hook on stdin as its own tool call, with the repository as `cwd`:

```
grep -rn cite docs/            -> ALLOW
find docs/ -name *.txt         -> ALLOW
git grep -n impl -- docs/      -> ALLOW
Grep(pattern="x", path="docs") -> ALLOW
```

Each of those prints the content of the plans, the drafts and the reviewer rounds under the gauntlet base — the three kinds the hook's own docstring at `.claude/hooks/no-impl-reads.py:29-38` denies because "of the four kinds quote implementation citations", and the same three that `Read` is refused at `:208`.

The cause is the nesting, not a missing containment rule. `readable()`'s prefix test is correct for every path it is given; it is the layout that hands it an allowed ancestor. Two things follow from the cause rather than from the symptom: no reader needs to know what a sweep will walk into, and no new test is needed on any road, because once no allowed root contains a denied subtree the ancestor a sweep names is either allowed all the way down or denied outright.

The superseded plan took the other road and priced it, in its own "What it costs": "A lexical operand grammar is what costs here. It is guessing at a shell". That cost is what this plan removes.

## What the owner sees change

The gauntlet's artifacts live at `gauntlet/` at the repository root, in six directories: `gauntlet/plans/approved`, `gauntlet/plans/drafts`, `gauntlet/specs/approved`, `gauntlet/specs/drafts`, `gauntlet/reviews` and `gauntlet/verdicts`. Reviews and verdicts stay flat, since neither has a draft stage.

A blind agent reads `docs/`, `tests/` and `state/` whole, plus `gauntlet/specs/approved/` and the documentation files at the repository root. `grep -rn cite docs/` is allowed again and prints no gauntlet artifact, because none is under `docs/` any more. `Grep(path="docs")` is allowed on the same ground. A read or a sweep rooted at `gauntlet/`, `gauntlet/plans/`, `gauntlet/plans/drafts/` or `gauntlet/reviews/` is denied, and the denial names `gauntlet/specs/approved/` as the subtree that is the agent's own.

Every agent that writes an artifact writes it one directory deeper: the `gauntlet-prosecutor` to `gauntlet/plans/approved/<slug>.txt`, the `gauntlet-arbiter` to `gauntlet/specs/approved/<slug>.txt`, the `gauntlet-juror` to `gauntlet/verdicts/<slug>.txt`, and both reviewers to `gauntlet/reviews/`. Drafts move under their own stage rather than into a shared drafts directory: `gauntlet/plans/drafts/<slug>.txt` and `gauntlet/specs/drafts/<slug>.txt`.

A consumer copying `.claude/` gets the new layout, and creates `gauntlet/` rather than a directory under `docs/` on first use.

## The open decision

The explicit denial is kept. `GAUNTLET_BASE` becomes `"gauntlet"`, `GAUNTLET_SPECS` becomes `"gauntlet/specs/approved"`, and both ordered tests at `.claude/hooks/no-impl-reads.py:206-208` stay where they are, in the order the comment at `:199-205` gives for them.

What the denial buys is the guarantee the docstring states at `:37-38` — "no `blind-reads.json` entry can re-open the" plans, the drafts or the rounds — and case 10 of the self-test pins it. Dropping the constants would leave blindness resting on `gauntlet/` merely being absent from `DEFAULT_ALLOW`, which a single per-repo `allow` entry reverses, and the hook concatenates the configured entries onto `DEFAULT_ALLOW` at `:154`, with no bound on what they may name. Two constants and two branches that already exist are a cheap price for a guarantee that holds against repo data.

This is the harmless direction of the invariant: an allowed leaf, `gauntlet/specs/approved/`, inside a denied tree, `gauntlet/`. A sweep rooted at the denied tree is denied; a sweep rooted at the allowed leaf is allowed and reaches nothing else.

## What it costs

The whole tree moves at once. There is no staged version of this change: the constants, the hooks, the agents, the scripts, the prose and the directories are one commit, because any split leaves a lane hook guarding a directory that is not where the artifacts are.

An approved spec block is overturned and re-specified, which costs a stage-2 round and a `gauntlet-scrivener` pass over the test lines listed above, across three test files. That is the largest single cost, and it is unavoidable: those tests assert the old layout by path.

Chains in flight are the largest operational cost, and there are two of them. `git status --porcelain` measures the main checkout alone and reaches no worktree, so it is the wrong instrument here; `git worktree list` returns eight trees under `.claude/worktrees/`, one per `spec/<slug>` branch, and `ls state/red` returns six slugs. The two with no red run are `blind-bash` — live at HEAD, `8541e42 Merge branch 'spec/blind-readonly' into impl/blind-bash` — and `pair-sh`, whose approved spec is tracked in the main checkout at HEAD (`git ls-files` lists it) while its worktree branch `spec/pair-sh` sits at `c8a9a63` and predates the current roster — `git ls-tree -r --name-only spec/pair-sh` lists `.claude/agents/gauntlet-accountant.md` and `.claude/agents/gauntlet-testsmith.md`, two agents the roster no longer has. That branch is stale rather than mid-flight; the slug's missing red run is what puts it on this list.

A worktree cut before the move carries its own copy of `.claude/` and its own artifacts at the old paths, so it stays self-consistent inside itself. The break is at the merge: a branch merged into main after the move re-creates an artifact under `docs/`, which restores exactly the nesting this change exists to remove, and `scripts/pair.sh` in the moved main looks for the spec at the new path while the branch holds it at the old one. So the move carries a precondition rather than a hope: each live branch either finishes and merges before the move lands, or takes the same `git mv` on its own branch before it is merged. The eight branches are named by `git worktree list`, and the two slugs above take the two halves of that precondition. `blind-bash` is live at HEAD and finishes and merges before the move lands, since its implementation branch is the one currently in flight. `pair-sh` needs nothing of its own: its approved spec is already tracked in main and moves with the rest, and its worktree branch is stale rather than in flight, so it is not merged — and if it ever is, it takes the `git mv` on the branch first, like any other unmerged branch.

A consumer mid-chain takes the same precondition, which is what the `CHANGELOG.md` entry says.

History is not moved, and a pre-move artifact stays readable at its old path. `git show <pre-move-rev>:<old plans path>` returns an approved plan's text after the move as before it, and with `docs/` allowed all the way down again `readable()` allows that read: `_git_candidates()` at `.claude/hooks/no-impl-reads.py:301-304` strips the revision prefix so the old path is the one scored, and the old base matches neither ordered test once `GAUNTLET_BASE` is `gauntlet`. The reach is real rather than theoretical, since the `gauntlet-arbiter` holds `Bash` at `.claude/agents/gauntlet-arbiter.md:4` with no `blind-bash.py` over it. It is accepted on the owner's word — "no one cares about reading old plans" — and no history-only denial is added, because one would re-nest a denial inside `docs/` and still leave a revision-scoped sweep open. The docstring at `.claude/hooks/no-impl-reads.py:29-38` states the denial as working-tree shaped, so a later reader does not take the guarantee for wider than it is.

One defect found while drafting and not fixed here: `.claude/hooks/blind-bash.py:9` states the `gauntlet-arbiter` has no `Bash`, which `.claude/agents/gauntlet-arbiter.md:4` falsifies. It is one stale docstring sentence and not a path this move re-spells. Which of the two is wrong was put to the owner twice and answered neither time, so this plan proceeds on the assumption that the frontmatter governs: the `gauntlet-arbiter` keeps its `Bash`, the docstring sentence is the stale half, and correcting it is its own chain. Nothing in this plan depends on the answer — the history residue is accepted either way.

Left out, on the owner's words — "Dropped from the old plan" — the lexical operand grammar, the `-e`/`--include=` handling, the `Grep`-road containment call site and the new refusal string. Also left out: the `scripts/pair.sh` blob-SHA defect, which has its own chain and is untouched here.
